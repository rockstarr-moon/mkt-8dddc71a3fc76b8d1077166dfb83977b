Rockstarr Orchestrator Quickstart

v0.7.0

Before your first session

  • A scaffolded workspace at /rockstarr-ai/ with your approved style guide and stack in place (your Rockstarr lead handles this).
  • The specialist plugins you use already installed — the lead coordinates them; it doesn't replace them. The SEO/GEO Engine play, for example, drives rockstarr-content.
  • 30 minutes with your Rockstarr lead to set your marketing goals and run your first play.
  • One decision in mind: what you most want to move first (more pipeline, more rankings, more authority). The lead works toward goals, so it helps to name them.

The one idea

Think of the orchestrator as your team lead. Instead of running each plugin yourself, you tell the lead what you want in plain language. It figures out which specialists to involve, does the safe internal work for you, and stops at anything that reaches your audience so you can approve it. It never publishes, sends, posts, or approves on its own.

First session with your Rockstarr lead (30 min)

  1. See where you stand: "Run the baseline audit" (or "what content do we already have?"). The lead inventories what already exists — blogs on your site, your LinkedIn newsletter editions, and the last 3 months of social — builds your master list (/06_reports/master-list.xlsx, long-form + social), and flags what's already been covered so the team never duplicates it. This is the starting point everything else builds on. Read-only — it records what's already public and publishes nothing.

  2. Set your goals: "Set our marketing goals." The lead interviews you for a one-page plan — what you're optimizing for across SEO/GEO, demand gen, and brand/authority, plus your priorities and constraints. Saved to /00_intake/marketing-plan.md.

  3. Get a status read: "How's the team doing?" The lead reads across every function and gives you one status — what's been produced, what's waiting on your approval, and how it ladders up to the goals you just set.

  4. Run your first play: "Get us ranking" (or "Run the SEO/GEO engine"). The lead audits your site, builds an SEO/GEO strategy and backlog, and drafts the first cluster pieces into your drafts folder — then stops and shows you what's waiting for approval. Nothing is published.

The autonomy line (how it stays safe)

  • It does automatically — the internal, reversible work that your audience never sees: audits, strategy and backlog, ideation, and drafting into /03_drafts/.
  • It always stops for you — anything audience-facing or irreversible: publishing, outreach sends, social posts, emails, CRM changes, and approval itself.

The lead stops at the first gate every time — even if you say "just do it." Approving and publishing stay your call.

What to type

Plain English, into a Cowork task, one at a time. Your own wording works too.

  • Where we stand (do this first): "Run the baseline audit." / "What content do we already have?" / "Build our master list." (inventories blogs + LinkedIn newsletter + recent social → /06_reports/master-list.xlsx, so nothing gets duplicated)

  • Set direction: "Set our marketing goals." / "Update the marketing plan." (writes /00_intake/marketing-plan.md)

  • Single pane (status): "How's the team doing?" / "What's waiting on me?" / "Where are we against our goals?" (runs team-report → /06_reports/team/)

  • Talk to the lead (do something): "Get us ranking." / "Grow my pipeline." / "What should the team focus on this week?" The lead interprets it, runs the matching play's safe steps, and stops at your first approval.

  • Run a play by name: "Run the SEO/GEO engine." Audit → strategy + backlog → first cluster drafts → stop at the publish gate. Or "spin the content flywheel" / "amplify our latest piece" — takes an approved long-form piece and stages social posts + a newsletter mention from it, then stops at your approval. Or "grow my pipeline" / "book more calls" — sharpens your target list, drafts the campaign, stages the outreach batch, drafts any waiting replies, and preps any booked calls, then stops before anything sends. Or "build our authority" / "establish our POV" — drafts a thought-leadership piece on your theme and stages the social post + poll to carry it, then stops at your approval.

  • Hands-off weekly planning (optional, off by default): "Turn on team autopilot." Once a week the lead works your highest-priority goal gap on its own — runs the safe steps and leaves the approvals waiting for you, then emails you a summary. "Turn off team autopilot" / "is team autopilot on?" to control it. It needs your marketing goals set first, and it always stops at the same gates.

  • Any question: Just describe what you want. The lead will tell you what it can do, what it would run, and where it would stop.

Your rhythm

  • At the very start, once. Run the baseline audit so the team knows what already exists before it plans or creates anything.

  • At onboarding / quarterly, 30 min. Set or refresh your marketing goals so the lead knows what to work toward.

  • Weekly, 10 min. "How's the team doing?" — clear the consolidated approval queue and see progress vs goals in one place.

  • On demand. Point the lead at a goal ("get us ranking") whenever you want to kick off coordinated work. The specialists' own autopilots keep the cadence going from there.

  • Or hands-off. Turn on team autopilot and the lead does the weekly kickoff itself (Monday mornings by default) — you just clear the approval queue it leaves you. Off by default; turn it on when you trust the rhythm.

If something seems off

  • The lead published or sent something without asking. It shouldn't — ever. That's a bug; tell your Rockstarr lead immediately.

  • A play stopped early. That's by design — it hit an audience-facing gate (usually "approve these drafts" or "publish"). Check /03_drafts/ for what's waiting and approve from there.

  • "It drafted a lot." A play is bounded to one cadence batch (your blogs-per-month). If you want more or less, change the cadence in stack.md.

  • The lead asked a clarifying question instead of acting. Good — it won't guess on an ambiguous request, and it will never improvise an audience-facing action to resolve doubt.

  • The lead says a play needs something first (e.g. an approved style guide, or content cadence is 0). Fix the prerequisite rather than working around it.

  • Anything else. Call your Rockstarr lead.

Your safety net

  • The lead never crosses an audience-facing gate — no publishing, sending, posting, emailing, CRM changes, or approvals, regardless of how you phrase the request.
  • It dispatches the real specialist skills; it doesn't reinvent their work, so every quality guard (stop-slop, the SEO/GEO checklist, the TL rubric) still runs exactly as it would if you ran the specialist yourself.
  • "How's the team doing?" is strictly read-only — it reports, it never changes anything.
  • Plays are bounded so they can't flood your approval queue.
  • Weekly autopilot (when on) holds off when your queue is already deep — it keeps the team moving, it won't bury you — and it stops at the same gates as everything else.
  • Nothing reaches your audience without your explicit approval.
